The living white paper separates implementation from evidence and proposals. A feature's presence in source does not imply production readiness.

## Development implementation

- Programmable account state, lifecycle roles and configurable authorization.
- Browser transaction witnesses, COSE intents and mixed authorization policies.
- Amount-based approval tiers and optional fixed daily/weekly shared budgets.
- Local dashboard, public account locators and an iPhone COSE companion.
- Independent fee-wallet funding for authorized transactions.

## Qualification work

Complete real-delay recovery and clean-device restoration evidence. Expand adversarial testing and wallet/device compatibility coverage. Independent audit and production operational design remain necessary.

## Efficiency and usability

Reduce onboarding reference-script deposits and transaction count, improve transaction size and execution costs, and make approval transport more seamless. Current DevKit cost figures are an implementation baseline, not a production fee promise.

## Proposed infrastructure

Sponsored intent execution could let users approve with familiar devices while providers handle transaction construction, fees and submission. Decentralized discovery, private transport, provider incentives and reliable fallback paths require additional design and qualification.

Additional authorization modules must prove that their cryptography and trust model can actually be enforced. Passkeys, social-login verification and hardware authentication are not claimed as current on-chain features.
